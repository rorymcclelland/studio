# 0017 Ad sizes drawn on request, and 2K pictures

**Date:** 2 November 2026. **Status:** accepted (built in Sprint 7: C7-1, with Rory's answers Q1 and Q2).

**Context.** Until Sprint 7 every ad was a 1080 × 1080 square (decision 0012), drawn on request from its `ad_variants` row (0014). A marketer needs the same ad in each size its channel uses. Rule 8 says words, logos and layout are composed in code; rule 1 says simulated pictures are labelled wherever shown; rule 7 says every read goes through the workspace's access check. Pictures were 1K (1024 × 1024), so a 9:16 crop was enlarged about 1.9 times.

**Decision.**
- **Sizes are data** (`src/lib/creative/render/sizes.ts`): paid social 1:1 1080 × 1080, 4:5 1080 × 1350, 9:16 1080 × 1920 and 1.91:1 1200 × 628; display 300 × 250 (medium rectangle), 300 × 600 (half page) and 728 × 90 (leaderboard) (Q2). Each names its channel, the layouts it draws and a phrase for refusals. A size's id is its pixel size (`728x90`).
- **Specs per layout and size** (`layoutSpec(layout, size)` in `layouts.tsx`). The 1080 square keeps Sprint 5's numbers and is pixel-identical to before (a golden test hashes the decoded pixels of thirteen renders made with the unchanged code). The portrait sizes keep the square's type sizes with more room; the landscape scales type and fixed parts by 0.72. Split puts the square picture whole in a square box (above the words in portrait, beside them in landscape); Card does the same (stacked in portrait, the logo beside the button; beside the words in landscape). Full bleed covers the canvas with a crop of the picture taken by sharp's attention strategy. The banners have one compact layout: headline, button, logo and legal lines, no body; the logo sits on the picture's corner in the medium rectangle and half page, and in the row in the leaderboard.
- **Pictures are never stretched.** The renderer crops or sizes the picture to its box with sharp before Satori draws it at one to one. The square's picture is passed as before. The checks record the picture's size, its box, how it was fitted (`cover`, `whole` or `as before`) and how much it was enlarged.
- **Readable floors.** Legal lines are never smaller than 22 px in the square (S5-3), the square's floor scaled with the canvas in the landscape (16 px), and 10 px in a banner. A size whose words or legal lines don't fit at its smallest step is refused with a plain reason ("the legal lines don't fit a 728 × 90 leaderboard at a readable size"); the other sizes are unaffected. Rory chose refusal over a shortened legal line (Q2), which is a compliance decision for a real brand.
- **Which layout draws a size.** The square draws only the ad's own layout (as `/ads/<id>.png` always has). Other social sizes try the ad's layout first, then the size's others, and the Sizes view says when another drew it. Display sizes draw the banner layout.
- **Served on request** at `/ads/<id>/<size>.png` (`/ads/<id>` and `/ads/<id>.png` stay the square). The same access check as `/ads/<id>` runs first; an unknown size, a size from the other channel, another workspace's ad, a signed-out request and a malformed id are the same 404. The size is in the ETag (with `SIZES_VERSION`, bumped when a non-square size draws differently); the square's ETag is unchanged, so browsers keep their copies. A refused size is a 422 with the reason. Drawings, their checks and refusals are cached in-process by ETag, bounded by count (160) and bytes (96 MB), so the Sizes view and its thumbnails draw each size once per instance. No table changed and no migration was needed: `ad_variants` stays one row per ad.
- **The Sizes view** is a page per ad (`/campaigns/<id>/ads/<ad>/sizes`, linked from each ad card), in its own component (`sizes-view.tsx`): a thumbnail per size opening the drawing full size, with its checks, or the reason it was left out. It notes "Upscaled from a 1K picture" where a picture is enlarged more than 1.1 times (every 1K picture is enlarged 1.05 times in the square, which is how every ad looked before; that isn't flagged).
- **Pictures at 2K** (Q1): the image model config draws 2K (2048 × 2048) from now on, at $0.0504 a picture instead of $0.0336 (3.98p instead of 2.65p). Make creative's typical cost rises from 14.7p to 19.1p, which the button rounds up to "about 20p, at most 41p" (the worst case, every picture redrawn and every call repaired, was 31p); New picture rises from "about 3p" to "about 4p". The estimates come from `prices.ts` and the configured size, so `costs.ts` needed no change beyond its inputs. Existing 1K pictures still render in every size. The mock image model draws at the configured size too (so tests and previews store 2K pictures). `toWebp` keeps a stored picture within the assets table's 2 MB limit: quality 82 first (a photo-like 2K picture is about 1 MB), then 72 and 60, then a smaller picture, rather than failing the insert after the picture was paid for.

**Consequences.** Every size's first view costs a drawing on whichever server instance answers; measured locally, 1080 × 1920 takes about half a second to a second depending on layout, banners about 0.1 to 0.3 s. A Sizes page draws four (or three) sizes before it answers, so it takes a few seconds the first time. The attention crop can cut a subject badly on some pictures; Rory sees every size before packing (C7-3). The picture check (Haiku) now reads 2K pictures, which the API reduces to about 1,600 tokens, as estimated. Decisions for languages (C7-2) and the launch pack (C7-3) build on these sizes.
