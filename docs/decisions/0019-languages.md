# 0019 Languages: per-brand languages, the Localise agent, and translated ads drawn from the English ad

**Date:** 2 November 2026. **Status:** accepted (built in Sprint 7: C7-2, with Rory's answers Q3, Q4 and Q5).

**Context.** Until Sprint 7 every ad was in English. A picked ad's words should go out in the brand's other languages, checked in every size of their channel (C7-1, decision 0018), and labelled as machine translations (rule 1). Legal lines are regulated text. Rule 3 says people approve consequential moves; rule 4 that every run is traced; rule 5 that every model call goes through `generate()`; rule 7 that every business table carries `workspace_id` with row-level security; rule 8 that words are drawn in code.

**Decision.**
- **Each brand picks its languages** (Q3) from a supported list in code (`SUPPORTED_LANGS` in `src/lib/creative/languages.ts`): French, German and Spanish to start, Latin script only (Satori can't shape right-to-left or complex scripts). A new table, `brand_languages` (`workspace_id`, row-level security, the brand referenced by id and workspace), holds one row per brand and language with that language's **legal lines and banned terms, written by a person** (Q4). The language tag is checked by pattern, not by list, so the next languages need no migration. A brand's Languages page (`/brands/<id>/languages`, linked from the brand page's Legal section) chooses them and edits the lines; the English banned terms stay banned in every language.
- **Legal lines are never machine-translated.** A language with no legal lines is refused for the brand's ads while the English kit has some (test case 5): the Translate button and the gallery's Languages bar say why, and a run asked for it (an old tab) leaves it out with the reason before any model call. When only some languages are refused, the button still works and the reason for each refused one is visible text beside it ("German is left out: ..."), not only a tooltip. A brand with no English legal lines may translate without any.
- **The demo brands' languages were written by Claude**, once, for the demo (Q4), and are marked `written_for_demo` ("Written for the demo" on the Languages page and in the gallery's note). They follow the English lines one for one and are kept close to the English lengths: a first French draft of Quillmoor's lines was too long for the square's Full bleed at its smallest legal size, so it was shortened. A person's edit of a language clears the mark. New demo loads get them; a demo brand loaded before C7-2 gets them with one click on its Languages page (no data in the migration).
- **Translations are copy sets.** `copy_sets` gains `lang` (default `en-GB`, so every set made before is English), `translated_from` (the English set) and `simulated` (the mock model wrote it). A translation is a new row, append-only like the others (the guard now covers the new columns); a trigger allows only translations of an English paid-social or display set of the same campaign, version, channel and audience; a partial unique index keeps one current translation per set and language. The Copy lists, Make creative and composition read English sets only.
- **A translated ad is not stored.** `ad_variants` stays one row per (English) ad, and its words must be English (the parts trigger now refuses a translated set). An ad in another language is drawn on request from the English ad's picture, layout and frozen kit with the current translation of its words and that language's legal lines: `/ads/<id>/<size>.png?lang=de-DE`, through the same access check as every size (another workspace's ad, an unsupported language and a language the ad has no words in are the same 404). Its ETag is built from the translated set, so each language revalidates on its own. Several ads share one set of words (four pictures each), so one translation serves them all: one model call per set and language. The gallery's thumbnail of a translation is the first size of the channel its words fit (the launch pack's fits, shared in `fit-cache.ts`), at that size's own width and height; when no size fits, it shows the reason instead (review fix 8). The Sizes view takes `?lang=` and switches between the ad's languages; the C7-3 launch pack can list picked ads × sizes × languages from the same data.
- **The Localise agent** (`src/lib/agents/localise.ts`, Creative squad) runs on **Sonnet** (Q5), at about 0.5p a set and language typically (1,800 tokens in, 250 out; the button rounds up, and shows the worst case with a repair each). Input: the English words, the channel (and its limits), the brand voice and rules, the language and its banned terms (with the English ones), claims needing evidence, real names from the market notes, products and the language's legal lines (shown so the words leave them out). Output: the three fields. Its mock answer returns fixed translations (a phrasebook for the Copy agent's mock words, otherwise fixed words per language and channel), saved as simulated and badged "Machine translation (simulated)".
- **Checks, with one repair** (`translationProblems` in `copy-rules.ts`): the channel limits in characters (a soft hyphen doesn't count); figures kept as the English has them (values only, since units are words in another language) and none added; the language's and the English banned terms and the claims that need evidence, with accents folded so "securite" and "sécurité" match; real company names; the brand-name heuristic for capitalised words except in German (every German noun has a capital); no quotation marks of any kind, and no first person outside the button in English or the language (je, mon, the elided j' and m' as in "J'ai" and "m'a" with either apostrophe, ich, mein, meins, yo, mi, mío and so on; review fix 1); no legal line repeated. The patterns for attributions, quantities in words and the brand-name heuristic were written for English and catch less in other languages; the "Machine translation" badge's explanation says so.
- **Fit in every size, with one repair.** The Translate workflow (`localise-workflow.ts`) adds a fit check to the agent's checks for this run: for every current ad on the set (each distinct layout and kit), every size of its channel is fitted as `drawInSize` would draw it (the square in the ad's own layout; other sizes in its layout first, then the size's others), without drawing (`fitAd`, the renderer's decisions split from its drawing). A size the English ad fills but the translation overflows sends the repair, naming the field and size ("the German headline is too long for a 1:1 (1080 × 1080) square: shorten it"). If the words still overflow after the repair, that size is left out with the renderer's reason and the rest are kept (the agent's `salvage`); the Sizes view refuses it as it does for English. A size the English ad can't fill (the Quillmoor leaderboard's legal lines, say) is left out in every language without a repair. Words that still break a rule after the repair are refused, and that language is left out for that set with the reason. In practice the size a long German word overflows is the square: C7-1's other sizes fall back to another layout (4:5 holds a 40-character compound in Card), so the story's "4:5" example became the square in the tests; the mechanism is the same for every size.
- **Soft hyphens.** The agent may mark break points in long words with U+00AD. The wrapper (`fit.ts`) ignores them in widths, breaks a word wider than its box at them with a visible hyphen at the line's end, and, if a text needs more lines than allowed at a size, tries once more breaking a word at a soft hyphen to fill the line before stepping down a size. Text without soft hyphens wraps exactly as before (the golden square is unchanged). A piece still wider than its box fails the fit check, as any word did.
- **latin-ext fonts.** Each bundled font's latin-ext file is read beside the Latin one and traced into the Vercel bundle. Satori takes one file per family name and weight and ignored a second file under the same name (the first try drew empty boxes for Polish letters), so the latin-ext files are registered as "<family> Ext" and the layouts draw with a stack (`Inter, Inter Ext`); text is measured with the Latin file falling back to latin-ext glyph by glyph. French, German and Spanish need only the Latin subset; the next Latin-script languages (Polish, Czech, Welsh) need no font change. Characters outside both are still refused rather than fetched.
- **Starting a translation** (rule 3): **Translate** on a picked ad, or **Translate the picked ads**, each with its cost before the click, like New words and Make creative. The click is the person's choice; nothing is published, replaced or spent beyond the AI cost, and the English ads are unchanged, so there is no separate approval step. One translation runs per campaign at a time. The run is traced like every agent run (a system step, then one model step per set and language, each with its attempts, problems, tokens and cost) and goes through `generate()` and the daily cap; when the cap is reached the run stops, keeps what was translated and lists what wasn't started.
- **Rule 1 everywhere.** Each translated ad shows "Machine translation" (or "Machine translation (simulated)") in the gallery, its "About" list and every size in the Sizes view, with an explanation that a model wrote it, the legal lines are the brand's own, and the English-only checks catch less. `MACHINE_TRANSLATION` and `translationBadge` are exported for the launch pack's manifest and README (C7-3).

**Consequences.** A translation is a few seconds per language and costs about 0.5p on Sonnet (the story estimated 1 to 2p); four picked ads on one set of words into three languages are three calls. Changing an ad's English words (New words, Edit words) moves it to a new set with no translations, so it is translated again; the old translations stay in the history. A person can discard one translation (Discard under its thumbnail; every ad on those words loses it) and Translate writes it again; a translated set can't be hand-edited yet (the copy-list actions refuse a translation's id: "Translations can't be edited or rewritten; discard it and translate again."), and discarded translations aren't listed under Discarded (there is nothing to restore that Translate can't write again; restoring one while its language has a current translation is refused in plain words).

**Removing a language.** Translations are snapshots: a language later removed from the brand (or its legal lines edited) leaves the translations already written as they are, still labelled "Machine translation" with the legal lines they were written with, shown on their ads and included in the launch pack (marked as written for the demo while those are the demo's lines). They go only when a person discards them. Translate no longer offers the removed language.

**Rolling back.** Sprint 6 code doesn't know `lang`: its copy lists read every set of the signed-off version, so a current translation would appear as English words of its own ("Ad words without ads", with Edit and New words). Before deploying Sprint 6 code over this schema, discard every translation (`update public.copy_sets set discarded_at = now() where translated_from is not null and discarded_at is null`, through the Neon connector); the rows and columns can stay. Discarded translations then show among the discarded sets in Sprint 6's lists, where Restore must not be used (the guard would allow it). Human review of translations is out of scope (the story), as are search, email and push words, right-to-left and complex scripts. Decimal commas ("12,5 %") read as a different figure from "12.5%" and would be refused; none of the demo words have one.
